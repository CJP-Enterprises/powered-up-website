# Getting a Raynham Home Ready for Generator Installation

The biggest thing that makes a generator install go smooth is the prep work that happens before the unit ever shows up. Where it sits, how the gas gets to it, and how it ties into your panel all get sorted first. Get those right and the actual install day is the easy part.

Here is how I walk through it when a Raynham homeowner calls me about backup power.

## Why do Raynham homes need backup power?

We lose power out here more than people expect. Nor'easters roll through in winter, and summer storms drop tree limbs on the lines. If you are off Route 44 or tucked into one of the wooded neighborhoods near Borden Colony, you know the drill when the wind picks up.

A generator keeps your heat, your well pump, your fridge, and your sump pump running when the grid goes down. For a lot of folks around here, the sump pump is the real reason. A wet basement in a March storm costs a lot more than a few flickering lights.

Whether you want the whole house covered or just the essential circuits changes the size of the unit and the scope of the work. That is one of the first things we sort out.

## Where does the generator go?

A standby generator needs a spot outside on a level pad, close enough to your gas supply and your electrical panel to keep the runs sensible. It also needs clearance around it for airflow and service access, and it needs to sit away from windows and doors so exhaust is not drifting back into the house.

On a lot of Raynham properties, the trick is finding a spot that hits all of those and does not put the unit right off your patio where you have to listen to it. I walk the yard, look at where the panel is, where the meter is, and where the gas comes in, then figure out the cleanest path for everything.

Drainage matters too. You do not want the pad sitting in a low spot that holds water every time it rains.

## What has to happen before install day?

A few pieces come together, and some of them are not my part of the job:

- **The pad.** The generator sits on a level base. That gets set before the unit lands.
- **The fuel.** Most standby units around here run on natural gas or propane. A plumber or gas fitter handles the fuel line and sizing so the unit gets the volume it needs. If you are on propane, your tank has to be sized for the load.
- **The electrical.** This is my part. The generator ties into a transfer switch, which is what safely swaps your house from grid power to generator power and back. That switch connects into your panel.
- **The panel itself.** If your service is older or already full, we may need to talk about a [panel upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades) before the generator has a clean place to land.

I coordinate with the gas side so we are not tripping over each other, and so everything is ready when it is ready.

## Does my electrical panel need work first?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. It depends on the age and capacity of your service and how much of the house you want backed up.

If you have got a modern panel with room in it, the transfer switch integration is straightforward. If you are working with an older setup, or the panel is already packed and you have also got an EV charger you want to add down the road, it is worth handling that at the same time.

That is actually a common conversation these days. A ton of people planning generator installation in Raynham MA are also looking at adding a car charger in the garage. Both of those lean on your panel, so if you know both are coming, it makes sense to plan the electrical once instead of twice.

I will look at your panel, your load, and what you are trying to run, and tell you straight whether it handles what you want or needs attention first.

## What about permits and inspection?

Plan on this being permitted work. What exactly is required for your address, and how the inspection is scheduled, is something the Town of Raynham handles, and they can confirm the specifics before you commit.

I pull what needs pulling and coordinate the inspection as part of the job. You should not have to chase paperwork on your own backup power.

## What drives the cost?

There is no honest way to quote a generator install sight unseen, and I will not throw a number at you over the phone that turns out wrong. What moves the price is the size of the unit, how much of the house you want covered, how far the runs are from the panel and gas supply, whether your panel needs work first, and what the fuel side takes.

Every one of those swings the number. Once I have walked your property and we have talked through what you actually need running, I can give you a real quote instead of a guess.
