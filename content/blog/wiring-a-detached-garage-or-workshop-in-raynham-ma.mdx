# Wiring a Detached Garage or Workshop in Raynham MA

If you want power out to a detached garage or workshop, what you're really after is a subpanel fed from your main house panel. That means a buried feeder cable, a panel out in the garage, and the right number of breakers for whatever you plan to run out there. I do this work all over Raynham, and the job lives or dies on how the feeder gets run and how the panel is sized.

Here's how I think through it before I ever pull wire.

## What actually goes into the job?

A detached garage feed has a few pieces that all have to line up. There's the main panel side, where I tap in and land a new breaker to feed the garage. There's the run itself, from the house out to the garage. And there's the subpanel on the far end that splits that power into circuits.

The run is usually the part people underestimate. On most detached buildings the feeder gets buried in a trench between the house and the garage, in conduit, at the right depth. That trench is real work, and it changes based on your yard, whether there's ledge, and what's already buried out there.

Once the wire is in the garage, I land it on a subpanel and build out the circuits from there. Lights, receptacles, a dedicated circuit for a compressor or welder, whatever the space needs.

## How do I size the garage subpanel?

This is the question that matters most, and it's why I don't quote garage jobs over the phone. Sizing the feed and the subpanel depends entirely on what you're going to plug in.

A garage that's just lights and a few outlets is a different animal than a workshop running a table saw, a dust collector, and an air compressor at the same time. And if you're thinking about an EV charger out there, that's a big load I need to plan for from the start, not bolt on later.

So when I do garage subpanel wiring in Raynham, I ask what you run today and what you might run in five years. It's a lot cheaper to pull a slightly bigger feeder once than to trench the yard twice.

## Can my house panel even handle it?

Before I add a garage feed, I look at what your existing main panel is doing. Adding a whole building's worth of load to a panel that's already close to full is how people end up with nuisance trips and lukewarm coffee makers.

A lot of the homes around Raynham and Bridgewater have panels that were sized for the house as it was built decades ago, not for today's loads. If yours is tight on space or already loaded up, part of this project might be a [panel upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades) at the house before I run anything to the garage.

I'll open the main panel, check what breakers are in there, look at the service size, and tell you straight whether it can take the new feed or whether we deal with the panel first. That's an on-site answer, not a guess.

## What about running an EV charger in the garage?

This is the one I get asked about the most lately, and a detached garage is a great place for a charger if the wiring is done right.

The thing to understand is that an EV charger is a steady, heavy load. Your car pulls that current for hours at a stretch. If you're building out a garage subpanel anyway, sizing it to carry a charger from day one is the smart move. Then adding the charger later is a short job instead of another trench.

If you already know a charger is in your future, tell me up front. I'll size the feeder and the subpanel so the charger drops right in when you're ready.

## What's different about wiring a garage in Southeastern Mass?

A couple of local things shape how I do this work here.

First, the ground. A lot of properties around Raynham, Easton, and Taunton have ledge close to the surface. When you hit rock in a trench, the plan changes, and that's something I'd rather find out by looking at your yard than by promising you a smooth dig.

Second, the weather. A detached garage in New England swings cold in winter and damp in the shoulder seasons. That means the outdoor-rated connections, the conduit, and how the feeder enters both buildings all have to be done to handle water and freeze-thaw. Cutting corners there is how you get corrosion and problems down the road.

Here's what I usually walk through on site:

- Where the trench can realistically run, and what's in the way
- Your main panel's condition and available capacity
- What loads you want in the garage now and later
- Whether an EV charger is part of the plan
- How the feeder enters and exits each building

## Do I need a permit for this?

Wiring a detached structure is the kind of work that gets permitted and inspected. What's required for your specific address in Raynham is a question for the town, and they can confirm it before you commit to anything.

I handle the permitting and the inspection as part of doing the job right. I'm not going to leave you with wiring in the ground that never got signed off on.
