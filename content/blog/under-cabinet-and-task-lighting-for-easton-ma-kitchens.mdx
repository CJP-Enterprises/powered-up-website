# Under-Cabinet and Task Lighting for Easton MA Kitchens

If your counters sit in shadow every time you cook, the fix is usually under-cabinet lighting and a few well-placed task fixtures. Done right, it lights the work surface without glare and hides the wiring so you never see it. Here is how I approach it on Easton kitchens.

## Why does my kitchen feel dark even with the ceiling lights on?

Most kitchens rely on ceiling cans or a center fixture. The problem is that you stand between that light and the counter, so your own body throws a shadow right onto the spot you are chopping and reading recipes.

Task lighting solves that by putting light where the work happens. Under-cabinet strips light the counter directly. Small fixtures over a sink or in a peninsula cover the spots the uppers do not reach.

I see this a lot in older Easton homes near the Center and out toward Furnace Village, where the original layout never accounted for how people actually cook now. The ceiling light alone was never going to be enough.

## What are my options for under cabinet lighting in Easton MA?

The most common choice today is LED. It runs cool, which matters when the fixture sits inches from your hands and your countertop, and it comes in strip form or in individual puck-style lights.

Strips give you an even wash across the whole counter. Pucks throw distinct pools of light and work well when you only want to hit a couple of spots. I walk your kitchen and look at cabinet depth, how the uppers are built, and where you actually stand before I recommend one over the other.

Color temperature is the other big decision. A warmer light feels cozier and reads closer to older bulbs. A cooler, whiter light makes it easier to see what you are doing but can feel clinical. I usually bring samples so you can see both against your own counters instead of guessing from a box.

## How do you hide the wiring so it looks clean?

This is where the work either looks built-in or looks like an afterthought. The goal is no visible cords and no visible transformer sitting on your counter.

Here is how I handle it:

- Run the wiring inside the wall and up through the cabinet where I can, so nothing shows below the uppers
- Tuck the fixture behind the face rail of the cabinet so you see the light on the counter, not the strip itself
- Put the controls where they make sense, usually a switch or dimmer by the main entry so you are not reaching behind anything
- Feed the low-voltage runs cleanly back to a hidden driver rather than plugging a wall wart into an outlet

On a finished kitchen I fish the wire without tearing the place apart. On a remodel where the walls are already open, we plan the runs before the drywall goes back so everything is buried from day one. If your project is a full [kitchen or bath remodel](/services/remodel-wiring), that is the ideal time to build this in.

## What drives the cost of a lighting job like this?

The honest answer is that it depends on your kitchen, and I would rather look at it than throw out a number that turns out wrong.

A few things move it more than anything else:

- How many cabinet runs need lighting and how far apart they are
- Whether I can fish the wire through finished walls or have to open anything up
- The type of fixture and dimmer you want
- Whether you want everything on one switch or zoned into separate controls

Two kitchens the same size can land in very different places depending on how the cabinets are built and how the existing wiring is laid out. Come look at it with me and I will give you an exact number for your kitchen, not a guess.

## Does under-cabinet lighting need its own circuit or switch?

Sometimes yes, sometimes I can work off what is already there. It comes down to what is on the existing circuit and how much load it is already carrying.

If your kitchen is on an older panel and every circuit is already busy, that is worth knowing before we add anything. In a lot of the older housing stock around Easton and neighboring Bridgewater, the electrical was never sized for how much we run today. I check the panel as part of the walk-through so there are no surprises after the fixtures are in.

Dimming matters here too. Not every LED plays nicely with every dimmer, and a mismatch is what causes that annoying flicker and buzz. I match the driver and the dimmer so the lights come up smooth and stay quiet.

## How long does the install take?

For a straightforward run of under-cabinet lights in a finished kitchen, it is usually a same-day job. A larger kitchen with multiple zones, new controls, or wiring that has to be fished through tricky walls takes longer.

I will tell you what to expect for your specific kitchen when I look at it, including whether we need to move anything on the panel to make room. I would rather set the right expectation up front than have you wondering halfway through.
