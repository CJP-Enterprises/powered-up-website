# Dedicated Circuit Requirements for Home EV Chargers in Raynham

A home EV charger needs its own dedicated circuit. That means a wire running straight from your electrical panel to the charger with nothing else tapped off it, no outlets, no lights, no shared load. If you're thinking about charging in your garage here in Raynham, that dedicated line is the first thing I look at.

## Why does an EV charger need its own circuit?

A Level 2 charger pulls a steady, heavy load for hours at a time. That's different from a toaster or a hair dryer that runs for a few minutes. When a circuit carries that kind of sustained draw, it has to be built for it and nothing else can be fighting for the same wire.

Share that circuit with other things and you're asking for tripped breakers, warm wiring, and a charger that throttles back or quits. A dedicated line keeps the charger isolated so it gets clean, consistent power every night.

That's the whole reason the dedicated circuit rule exists. The charger is one of the biggest things you'll ever plug into your house, and it deserves its own path back to the panel.

## What determines the size of the circuit?

The charger you buy sets the target. Different units are rated to pull different amounts of current, and the circuit, the breaker, and the wire all have to match that rating. I size everything to the charger's spec, not the other way around.

Distance matters too. If your panel is in the basement and you park at the far end of the garage, that's a longer run, and a longer run can change the wire I use. I'd rather pull the right conductor once than have you deal with voltage drop later.

The last piece is your panel itself. I need to know what your service can actually support before I add a big new load to it. Some Raynham homes, especially older ones off Route 44 and the places built out decades ago, have panels that are already working hard.

## Will my panel handle it?

This is the question that decides a lot of EV jobs. Your panel has a finite amount of capacity, and every circuit already in the house is drawing from it. Adding a charger means there has to be room left over, both a free breaker slot and enough service capacity overall.

When I come out, I open the panel and look at what's in there, what your service size is, and how loaded things already are. If there's room, great, we add the circuit and move on. If the panel is full or the service is maxed out, we talk about a [panel upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades) before the charger goes in.

I'd rather tell you that up front than surprise you on install day. A charger on a panel that can't carry it is a problem waiting to happen, and it's not worth cutting corners on.

## What does the install actually involve?

Here's the general sequence when I do an ev charger installation in Raynham. It varies house to house, but the bones are the same.

- I check your panel and service capacity first, before anything else.
- I figure out the best route from the panel to where you park.
- I run the dedicated conductor, sized to your charger.
- I land a properly sized breaker in the panel for that circuit alone.
- I mount and connect the charger, then test it under load.

Where the run goes depends on your house. A basement panel with an attached garage is clean. An outdoor run, a detached garage, or a finished space I have to fish wire through takes more work. I'll walk it with you before I quote so you know exactly what we're dealing with.

Garages around here get cold. I keep that in mind with where and how I mount things, and I use materials rated for the conditions so a January night doesn't bother the connection.

## Do I need a permit for this in Raynham?

Plan on this being permitted electrical work. The specifics for your exact address and the inspection process are a question for the Raynham building and wiring inspector's office, and they can confirm what's needed before you commit to anything.

I handle the permitting side as part of the job so you're not chasing paperwork. The point of the inspection is that someone besides me confirms the work is safe, and that's a good thing when you're adding a load this size to your home.
