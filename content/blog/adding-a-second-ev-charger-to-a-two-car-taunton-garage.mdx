# Adding a Second EV Charger to a Two-Car Taunton Garage

Two EVs in one garage means two chargers, and the good news is your house can usually handle it. The real question isn't whether it's possible, it's how much room your electrical panel has left and how we run the second circuit without stepping on the first. That's what I look at before I quote anything.

## Can My Panel Handle a Second Charger?

This is the first thing I check, and it decides most of the job. A Level 2 charger pulls a real load, and two of them running at the same time is a lot of demand on one panel.

When I walk your garage in Taunton, I open the panel and look at what's already in it. I'm checking the size of your service, how many open slots you have, and how much of your capacity is already spoken for by the AC, the range, the dryer, and the first charger.

Sometimes there's plenty of room and it's a straightforward add. Other times the panel is already full or maxed out on load, and we need to talk about a panel upgrade or a load management setup before adding number two. I'll tell you which camp you're in on site, not over the phone with a guess.

## What's Load Management and Do I Need It?

Load management, or load sharing, is a way to run two chargers off capacity that couldn't otherwise support both at full power at once. The chargers talk to each other and split the available power, so when both cars are plugged in they share instead of tripping the main.

For a lot of two-car households, this is the smart move. Most people charge overnight, and even a shared setup fills both cars by morning. It can also save you from a bigger service upgrade if your panel is tight.

Whether it makes sense for you depends on your panel, how far you drive, and whether both cars are ever charging on the same clock. I'll lay out the tradeoff so you can decide with real information instead of a sales pitch.

## How Do You Run the Second Circuit in a Two-Car Garage?

Each charger needs its own dedicated circuit run from the panel, unless we're doing a load-sharing pair designed to share one feed. In a typical two-car garage, the two cars park side by side, so I plan the wire runs and mount locations so both cords reach their charge ports without stretching across the bay.

The route matters. If your panel is on the far wall from where the second car parks, that's a longer run, and in an older Taunton home with a finished garage or blown-in insulation, getting the wire cleanly from A to B takes more work than an open stud bay. I'd rather fish it neatly than staple conduit across your whole ceiling.

I also look at where you'd actually want the unit. Hardwired or plug-in, wall height, cord length, and keeping the connector out of the way when you're loading groceries or backing out. Small stuff, but it's the difference between a charger you like and one that annoys you every day.

A clean second EV charger installation in a Taunton MA garage comes down to those details: the circuit, the route, and the placement.

## What Drives the Cost?

I won't throw a number at you before I've seen the garage, because the things that move the price are all specific to your house.

Here's what I'm weighing:

- How much capacity your panel has, and whether it needs an upgrade first
- Whether we go with two dedicated circuits or a load-sharing pair
- The distance from the panel to the second parking spot
- Whether the run is through open framing or has to be fished through finished walls
- The charger itself, and whether you're supplying it or I am

Any one of those can swing the job. That's why an honest quote comes after I look, not before. If your panel needs work to make room, a [panel upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades) may be part of the conversation, and I'll be straight with you about that up front.

## Does the Massachusetts Winter Change Anything?

A little, yes. If your garage is attached and heated, the charger doesn't care much. But plenty of homes around Raynham, Bridgewater, and Easton have detached or unheated garages, and cold affects both charging speed and where I mount hardware.

I also pay attention to what's outside. If any part of the run touches an exterior wall or an unconditioned space, I use the right rated equipment and seal penetrations so you're not pulling cold air or moisture into the house.

The other winter factor is demand. Everyone's heat, cars, and everything else are pulling harder in January. That's part of why I look hard at your total load before adding a second charger, so nothing trips when you need it most.

## Are There Rebates for a Second Charger?

There have been utility and state programs tied to EV charging in Massachusetts over the years. The amounts, the rules, and what qualifies change, and eligibility often depends on your utility and your specific setup.

I'm an electrician, not the program office. Check with your electric utility and, if it's a tax question, your tax preparer, so you get the current rules for the year you install. I'll make sure the work itself is done to a standard that won't cause you trouble if you do apply.
