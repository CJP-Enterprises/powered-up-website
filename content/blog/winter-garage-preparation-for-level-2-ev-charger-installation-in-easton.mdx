# Winter Garage Preparation for Level 2 EV Charger Installation in Easton

If you're planning a Level 2 charger before the cold really sets in, the best thing you can do right now is get your garage ready so the install goes smooth. Most of the headaches I run into this time of year aren't about the charger itself. They're about a cold, cluttered, under-powered garage that nobody prepped.

Here's what I look at and what you can handle yourself before I show up.

## Why does winter change the way I approach a garage install?

In Easton, garages take a beating from November through March. A lot of the ones I work in are detached or sit under a bonus room, and they get cold enough that your hands stop working before the job's done.

Cold matters for the install itself. Wire and conduit get stiff and harder to bend clean when it's freezing, and that affects how the finished run looks on your wall. If your garage has any heat at all, even a little space heater running while I work, the whole thing goes faster and looks better.

The other winter factor is moisture. Snow melts off your car and pools on the floor, and that salt and water mix is rough on anything mounted low. I plan the mounting height and the location with that in mind.

## What should I clear out before you come to install?

The charger goes near your panel or near where your car parks, and I need clean access to both. The single most common delay I see is a garage packed to the walls where I can't get to the panel or run a ladder along the ceiling.

Before I come out, it helps if you:

- Clear the wall where you want the charger mounted
- Clear a path to your electrical panel
- Move anything stored directly in front of the panel door
- Think about which side you actually park on so the cable reaches

That last one matters more than people expect. The charger cable only reaches so far, and if you back in some days and pull in others, tell me. I'd rather mount it in a spot that works both ways than have you fighting the cord all winter.

## How do I know if my panel can handle it?

This is the part that decides whether your install is simple or turns into a bigger project. A Level 2 charger pulls real power, and your panel has to have room for it.

When I walk your garage, I open the panel and look at what's already in there. I'm checking how much capacity is left, whether there's physical space for the new breaker, and how far the charger location is from the panel. A short, clean run inside the garage is the easy case. A run that has to cross the house or go up into an attic is more involved.

If your panel is older or already full, you may be looking at a [panel upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades) before the charger can go in. That's not me upselling you. It's just that there has to be somewhere for the power to come from. I'll tell you straight which situation you're in once I've seen it.

## What does the actual EV charger installation involve?

Once the panel's sorted, the work itself is fairly direct. I set the breaker in the panel, run the wire to the charger location, mount the unit on the wall, and make the connections. If the run is exposed along a block or concrete garage wall, I use conduit so it's protected and looks clean.

For a hardwired unit, the charger connects straight to the circuit. For a plug-in unit, I install the right receptacle and you plug the charger into it. Which way we go depends on your charger and how you want it set up, and I'll walk you through the tradeoffs.
