# Wiring a New Construction Home in Bridgewater MA

The best time to think about how your house is wired is before the drywall goes up. Once the walls are open, I can put outlets, switches, lighting, and future runs exactly where you want them without tearing into anything later. That planning stage is where a good electrical layout is won or lost.

I do a lot of new construction wiring around Bridgewater, from the newer builds off Pleasant Street to homes going up on the outskirts toward Middleborough. Here's how I approach it.

## When does the electrical work actually start?

Electrical is a rough-in trade, which means my first real pass happens after the framing is up and before insulation and drywall. That's when I run the wire, set the boxes, and get everything in the walls where it belongs.

Before that, though, I like to walk the plans with you. I want to know how you actually live in a house. Where the TV goes, where you charge your phone at night, whether the island in the kitchen needs power, where you want lights on switches versus lights you'll never touch.

That conversation shapes everything. Moving an outlet on paper costs nothing. Moving it after the sheetrock is hung is a different story.

## What gets planned before the walls close up?

The rough-in is your one clean shot at putting wire where you want it. A few things I always push people to think through early:

- Kitchen and bath circuits, since those rooms pull the most and have the most code-driven requirements
- Lighting layout, including recessed cans, under-cabinet, and exterior fixtures
- Where the panel lives and whether it has room to grow
- Low-voltage and future runs, like running conduit or a spare line to a spot you might use down the road
- Outdoor power for the driveway, the deck, and anything you plan to add later

That last point matters more than most people realize. Pulling one extra wire during rough-in is cheap and easy. Adding it after the house is finished means fishing walls or running surface conduit.

## Should I plan for an EV charger now, even if I don't own one yet?

Yes. This is the single biggest thing I tell people building in southeastern Mass right now. Even if you're not driving electric today, the odds you or the next owner will are climbing fast.

The smart move during new construction is to set up the garage for a charger before the walls close. That means running a proper circuit from the panel to the garage wall and landing it where a charger would mount. If you're not ready to buy the unit yet, I can still get the wire and box in place so the future install is simple.

Doing it now, while the walls are open and the panel is fresh, is far easier than coming back later to add capacity. If you want to see how I handle those installs, here's more on my [EV charger work](/services/ev-chargers). Planning for it during the build is the version of that job that saves you the most headache.

## How does the panel factor into all this?

The panel is the heart of the whole system, and in a new build you get to size it right from the start. That's a luxury you don't have in an older home where I'm often trying to fit new loads into a box that's already full.

When I plan a panel for new construction, I'm not just covering what you need on move-in day. I'm thinking about the EV charger, a possible generator, a hot tub, a workshop, whatever you might add over the years. Leaving room in the panel now means you're not paying to upgrade it in five years.

Where the panel sits also matters. I want it accessible, out of the weather, and close enough to your heavy loads that the wire runs make sense. All of that gets decided during new construction wiring in Bridgewater MA, and it's a lot cheaper to get right the first time.

## What about generators and power outages out here?

Southeastern Massachusetts gets its share of weather. Nor'easters in the winter, the occasional hurricane remnant blowing up from the coast, and the ice storms that take lines down and leave neighborhoods dark for days.

If a standby generator is on your wish list, the build is the time to plan for it. I can rough in the transfer switch location, the gas line coordination with your plumber, and the wiring so the generator install later is straightforward instead of a rework.

Even if you're not installing one on day one, telling me now lets me leave the door open. A little forethought during rough-in means the future job is plug-and-go rather than a tear-up.

## What drives the cost of wiring a new house?

There's no honest way to give you a number without seeing the plans. The size of the house, the number of circuits, the lighting package, whether you're adding EV and generator provisions, and how the layout runs all move the price in real ways.

A simple three-bedroom ranch and a two-story with a finished basement, a workshop, and a fully lit exterior are completely different jobs. That's why I quote off the actual plans, not a guess.

Get me the drawings and walk me through how you want to use the space, and I'll give you a real number tied to your actual house.
