# Choosing Between Garage and Outdoor EV Charger Mounting in Bridgewater

If you've got a garage you can park in, mounting your charger inside is usually the simpler call. If you park in the driveway or don't have garage space, an outdoor mount works fine as long as the gear and the install are built for it. The right answer depends on where your car actually sits and where your panel is.

Let me walk through how I think about it when I'm standing in your driveway looking at the job.

## Should I mount my charger in the garage or outside?

Start with where you park. The charger wants to be close to where the car's charge port lands when it's sitting still. Dragging a cable across a whole driveway or around a parked car every night gets old fast, and it's hard on the cable.

If you pull into the garage, mounting inside keeps the unit out of the weather and usually keeps the wire run short, especially if your panel is in the garage or basement nearby. That's the clean setup when it's available.

If you park outside, an outdoor mount by the driveway makes more sense. The equipment has to be rated for outdoor use, and the connection and mounting have to be done so water never finds its way in. That's where the install quality matters more than anything else.

## How does New England weather change the outdoor decision?

Bridgewater winters are the real test. We get freeze-thaw cycles, wet snow, and the occasional ice storm that coats everything. An outdoor charger lives through all of that, so the housing, the cable, and the connector all need to handle cold and moisture.

Plugging and unplugging with cold hands in a snowbank off Pleasant Street in January is a factor people forget until they're doing it. A sheltered spot, even a small overhang, makes outdoor charging a lot more pleasant in the winter.

Inside the garage you skip most of that. The unit stays dry, the cable stays flexible because it's not frozen stiff, and you're not clearing snow off anything before you charge.

Either way, I look at how water drains near the mounting spot and whether the location takes a beating from wind-driven rain and snow. Those details decide how I flash and seal the install.

## What actually drives the install work?

The charger location is only half of it. The other half is getting power from your panel to that spot. A few things move the amount of work involved:

- How far the charger is from the panel, and what the wire has to pass through to get there
- Whether the run is an easy open path or has to go through finished walls, across a ceiling, or outside along the house
- The condition and capacity of your existing panel
- Whether the mounting surface is framed wall, masonry, or an exterior finish

A garage mount with the panel right there is about as straightforward as it gets. An outdoor mount on the far side of the house from the panel means a longer run and more weatherproofing, which is more labor.

This is also why I don't quote these over the phone without seeing it. The spread between an easy install and a hard one is wide, and it comes down to things I need to put eyes on. ## Will my electrical panel handle it?

This is the question that surprises people. An EV charger pulls a real load, and older panels around Bridgewater and the surrounding towns don't always have the room or capacity to add it cleanly.

When I come out, I check what your panel can take and how it's currently loaded. Sometimes there's room and it's a simple add. Other times the panel is already full or dated, and a charger install turns into a panel conversation first.

If that's the case, I'll tell you straight. It's better to find out before we start than to get halfway in and hit a wall. A panel that's maxed out isn't something you want to force a charger onto.

## Garage, outdoor, or a bit of both?

Some setups don't fall cleanly into one bucket. You might mount inside the garage but position the unit near the door so you can charge a second car parked in the driveway when you need to. That works when the cable reaches and you're okay with the door cracked.

I've also seen people want the charger outside now because they're planning a garage change later, or vice versa. If you tell me what you're thinking down the road, I can place the charger and run the wire so you're not redoing it in two years.

The point is there's no single right answer. It's your parking habits, your panel, your weather exposure, and how you want to live with it every day.
