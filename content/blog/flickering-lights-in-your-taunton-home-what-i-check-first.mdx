# Flickering Lights in Your Taunton Home: What I Check First

Most flickering comes down to one of three things: a loose bulb, a loose connection somewhere in the circuit, or something bigger at your panel or utility feed. The trick is figuring out which one you've got before it turns into a bigger problem. Here's how I sort it out.

## Is it one light or the whole house?

This is the first question I ask, and you can answer it before I even show up.

If a single fixture flickers, the problem is usually local to that fixture. Loose bulb, a bad socket, a dimmer that doesn't play nice with LED bulbs, or a loose wire behind the switch or in the fixture box. These are usually the easier fixes.

If lights flicker across multiple rooms at once, or the whole house dims when the fridge or furnace kicks on, that points to something upstream. Now we're talking about the panel, a main connection, or the service coming in from the street. That's the kind of flicker I don't want you ignoring.

## What I check first on a service call

When I walk in for flickering lights in Taunton MA, I don't start by pulling the panel apart. I start simple and work my way up, because the cheap fixes are also the most common.

- I check the bulbs and confirm they're rated for the fixture and dimmer they're in. LED and older dimmers are a frequent culprit.
- I check the switch and the fixture box for loose connections and backstab terminals that have worked loose over time.
- I check the affected circuit at the breaker for signs of heat, corrosion, or a breaker that's not seated right.
- If it's whole-house, I look at the main lugs, the neutral connections, and how the service is landing in the panel.

A lot of older homes around Taunton, especially the ones near the Weir and the older streets off Broadway, have panels and wiring that have seen decades of small changes. Every added circuit and every reused connection is a spot where something can loosen up. Heat cycling from our New England summers to winters doesn't help. Metal expands and contracts, and connections that were fine ten years ago start to back off.

## When flickering is actually a warning

Some flickering is annoying. Some is telling you something is wrong before it becomes dangerous. I take these seriously:

- Lights that dim hard when a large appliance starts, then recover slowly.
- Flickering that comes with a burning smell, warm switch plates, or buzzing.
- Browning or scorch marks around an outlet, switch, or the panel.
- Flickering that's gotten worse over weeks or months.

A loose connection at the wrong spot doesn't just flicker. It arcs, and arcing makes heat. That's how connections go from a nuisance to a real hazard. If you're seeing any of the signs above, stop using that circuit and call me. This is the core of what I do in [wiring repair and troubleshooting](/services/wiring-repair), and it's worth getting eyes on before it spreads.

## Could it be the utility and not my house?

Sometimes it isn't your wiring at all. If your whole street or your neighbors are seeing the same thing, or the flicker shows up at random times unrelated to your appliances, the issue can be on the utility side, in the drop or transformer feeding your area.

When I'm on site, part of what I'm doing is figuring out whether the problem lives inside your house or comes in from the street. If it's the utility's equipment, I'll tell you straight so you're calling the right people instead of paying me to chase something that isn't mine to fix.

That said, a lot of "utility" complaints turn out to be a loose main connection or a service that's undersized for how the home is being used now. Newer electric ranges, heat pumps, and EV chargers pull a lot more than the loads these older Taunton panels were set up for. If your service is stretched thin, flickering can be an early sign you're due for more capacity.

## Does flickering mean I need a bigger panel?

Not always. Plenty of flicker is a five-minute fix that has nothing to do with your panel size. I'd never sell you an upgrade you don't need.

But if I find that your service is undersized, your panel is full, or your connections are struggling under modern loads, I'll walk you through what a panel upgrade actually solves and what it doesn't. This comes up more and more as folks add EV chargers around Bridgewater, Raynham, and Easton. An older panel that was fine for years suddenly gets asked to do a lot more, and the lights are the first thing to complain.
