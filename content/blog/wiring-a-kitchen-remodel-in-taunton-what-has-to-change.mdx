# Wiring a Kitchen Remodel in Taunton: What Has to Change

Most kitchen remodels in Taunton need more electrical work than people expect. The old wiring was built for a kitchen from a different era, and once you move cabinets, add an island, or swap in a bigger range, the circuits behind the walls have to change with it. Here is what I look at and what tends to get added.

## Why can't I just reuse the old kitchen wiring?

Older kitchens around here, especially in the neighborhoods off Weir Street and the older homes near downtown, were wired for a lot less than a modern kitchen pulls. One or two outlets on a shared circuit fed the whole counter, and that was fine when the biggest thing plugged in was a toaster.

Now you have an air fryer, a coffee maker, a microwave, and a dishwasher all wanting power at once. Reusing the old runs means you either trip breakers constantly or you overload wiring that was never sized for the load.

When I open up the walls during a remodel, I usually find the existing runs cannot cover what the new layout needs. That is not a knock on whoever did it originally. The kitchen just asks for more now.

## What new circuits does a modern kitchen need?

This is where most of the work happens. A remodel almost always means pulling new home runs back to the panel. The exact list depends on your layout and your appliances, but here is what I am typically adding or upgrading:

- Dedicated circuits for the countertop receptacles so you can run multiple appliances without nuisance trips
- A dedicated circuit for the dishwasher and one for the disposal
- A separate line for the microwave, especially if it is a built-in or over-the-range unit
- A range or cooktop circuit sized to that specific appliance, gas or electric changes this a lot
- Refrigerator on its own circuit so a tripped breaker somewhere else does not spoil your food
- GFCI protection where it is needed and proper receptacle spacing along the counters

If you are adding an island, that is its own conversation. An island needs power, and how we route it depends on your slab, your floor joists, and whether we can get under the kitchen from a basement or crawlspace.

## Does a kitchen remodel mean I need a panel upgrade?

Sometimes. It depends on what your panel can still handle once we add these new circuits. If your existing panel is already full, or it is one of the older types I would rather not add to, we talk about that before the walls close up.

Adding a bunch of new kitchen circuits pulls load, and the panel has to have room for it, both physically and electrically. On some jobs there is plenty of headroom and we just add breakers. On others, the honest answer is that the panel is the real bottleneck.

I will tell you which one you are looking at after I see the panel and go through your appliance list. If an upgrade makes sense, I handle that too. You can read more about how I approach [panel work here](/services/panel-upgrades).

## When in the remodel does the electrical happen?

Timing matters, and it usually catches people off guard. Electrical happens in two phases.

The first phase is rough-in. This is after demo, once the walls are open and framing is set but before insulation and drywall. This is when I run all the new wire, set the boxes, and get everything positioned. It has to happen in the right window or you are opening up finished walls later, which nobody wants.

The second phase is trim-out. That is after cabinets, counters, and finishes go in. This is when I install the receptacles, switches, under-cabinet lighting, and hook up the appliances. Coordinating this with your cabinet installer and contractor keeps the job moving instead of stalling.

For kitchen remodel wiring in Taunton, I try to get in early on planning so we place everything around your actual cabinet and appliance layout, not a guess.

## What about lighting and the little details?

Kitchen lighting is where a remodel really shows. Recessed cans, under-cabinet strips, pendants over an island, and a good switch layout all get planned during rough-in because the wire has to be there before drywall.

I also look at things people forget until it is too late. Where do you want a charging spot for phones. Do you want a receptacle inside a cabinet for a hardwired appliance. Is there a spot on the counter that always ends up cluttered with a power strip. We solve those before the walls close, not after.

New England humidity and older Taunton homes also mean I keep an eye on existing grounding and any moisture-prone spots near the sink and dishwasher. If I find something worth correcting while the walls are open, that is the cheapest time to deal with it.

## What drives the cost of rewiring a kitchen?

I get this question early and it is a fair one. The honest answer is that it comes down to a handful of things I have to see before I can put a real number on it.

- How many new circuits your layout and appliances require
- Whether your panel has room or needs to be upgraded
- How hard the wire runs are, meaning can I get at them from a basement or am I fishing through finished walls
- The lighting plan and how many fixtures and switches are involved
- Whether we uncover anything behind the walls that should be fixed while we are in there

Those swing the price enough that quoting you sight unseen would just be a guess, and a guess does not help either of us. Once I walk the kitchen and go through your plans, I give you a real number.
